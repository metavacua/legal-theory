Appendix: Synoptic Chart of 100 Landmark First Amendment Cases

Case Name & Citation Year Decided First Amendment Clause(s) Key Doctrine or Test Established Summary of Holding
*Schenck v. United States*, 249 U.S. 47 1919 Speech Clear and Present Danger Upheld conviction for anti-draft leaflets, establishing that speech can be restricted if it poses a clear and present danger.
*Gitlow v. New York*, 268 U.S. 652 1925 Speech, Press Incorporation Doctrine Held that the First Amendment's protections of speech and press apply to the states through the Fourteenth Amendment.
*Whitney v. California*, 274 U.S. 357 1927 Speech, Assembly (Brandeis Concurrence) Upheld a conviction for syndicalism, but is famous for Brandeis's concurrence arguing speech can only be punished if danger is imminent.
*Stromberg v. California*, 283 U.S. 359 1931 Speech Symbolic Speech Struck down a state law banning red flags, recognizing symbolic acts as a form of protected speech.
*Near v. Minnesota*, 283 U.S. 697 1931 Press Prior Restraint Established a strong presumption against prior restraint, striking down a law that allowed enjoining "malicious" newspapers.
*De Jonge v. Oregon*, 299 U.S. 353 1937 Assembly Incorporation Doctrine Incorporated the freedom of assembly, holding that peaceful assembly for lawful discussion cannot be made a crime.
*Hague v. CIO*, 307 U.S. 496 1939 Assembly, Speech Public Forum Doctrine Invalidated a permit ordinance, establishing streets and parks as traditional public forums for speech and assembly.
*Cantwell v. Connecticut*, 310 U.S. 296 1940 Free Exercise Incorporation Doctrine Incorporated the Free Exercise Clause and struck down a licensing scheme for religious solicitation.
*Cox v. New Hampshire*, 312 U.S. 569 1941 Assembly, Speech Time, Place, and Manner Upheld a permit system for parades, allowing reasonable, content-neutral "time, place, and manner" restrictions.
*Chaplinsky v. New Hampshire*, 315 U.S. 568 1942 Speech Fighting Words Doctrine Upheld a conviction for insults, defining unprotected "fighting words" as those that inflict injury or incite a breach of the peace.
*West Virginia State Board of Ed. v. Barnette*, 319 U.S. 624 1943 Speech Compelled Speech Ruled that compelling public school students to salute the flag violates the First Amendment.
*Everson v. Board of Education*, 330 U.S. 1 1947 Establishment Incorporation; "Wall of Separation" Incorporated the Establishment Clause and invoked the "wall of separation," though it upheld transportation reimbursements for parochial school students.
*McCollum v. Board of Education*, 333 U.S. 203 1948 Establishment Released Time Found unconstitutional a "released time" program for religious instruction held within public school buildings.
*Terminiello v. Chicago*, 337 U.S. 1 1949 Speech Heckler's Veto Overturned a breach-of-the-peace conviction, holding that speech is protected even if it "stirs people to anger."
*Dennis v. United States*, 341 U.S. 494 1951 Speech Clear and Present Danger (modified) Upheld Smith Act convictions of communists, using a balancing test that weakened the "clear and present danger" standard.
*Zorach v. Clauson*, 343 U.S. 306 1952 Establishment Released Time Upheld a "released time" program where students left school grounds for religious instruction.
*Yates v. United States*, 354 U.S. 298 1957 Speech Advocacy of Doctrine vs. Action Narrowed the Smith Act, distinguishing between protected advocacy of abstract doctrine and unprotected advocacy of illegal action.
*NAACP v. Alabama ex rel. Patterson*, 357 U.S. 449 1958 Assembly Freedom of Association Protected the NAACP from having to disclose its membership list, recognizing a constitutional right to freedom of association.
*Torcaso v. Watkins*, 367 U.S. 488 1961 Establishment Religious Tests for Office Struck down a state requirement that public officeholders declare a belief in God.
*Engel v. Vitale*, 370 U.S. 421 1962 Establishment School Prayer Ruled that state-sponsored, non-denominational prayer in public schools is unconstitutional.
*Edwards v. South Carolina*, 372 U.S. 229 1963 Assembly, Speech Public Protest Protected peaceful civil rights marchers, holding states cannot criminalize the peaceful expression of unpopular views.
*NAACP v. Button*, 371 U.S. 415 1963 Petition, Assembly Litigation as Expression Protected the NAACP's right to sponsor civil rights litigation as a form of protected political expression and association.
*Sherbert v. Verner*, 374 U.S. 398 1963 Free Exercise Compelling Interest Test Held that denying unemployment benefits to someone who refused to work on her Sabbath burdened her religion and required a compelling state interest.
*Abington School District v. Schempp*, 374 U.S. 203 1963 Establishment School Bible Reading Struck down state-mandated Bible readings in public schools and established the "secular purpose and primary effect" test.
*New York Times Co. v. Sullivan*, 376 U.S. 254 1964 Speech, Press Actual Malice Standard Established that public officials must prove "actual malice" to win a defamation suit.
*United States v. O'Brien*, 391 U.S. 367 1968 Speech Expressive Conduct (*O'Brien* Test) Upheld a conviction for burning a draft card, creating a test for regulating expressive conduct.
*Pickering v. Board of Education*, 391 U.S. 563 1968 Speech Public Employee Speech Established a balancing test for the free speech rights of public employees.
*Tinker v. Des Moines*, 393 U.S. 503 1969 Speech Student Speech (*Tinker* Test) Affirmed students' free speech rights in public schools, allowing restriction only for "material and substantial disruption."
*Brandenburg v. Ohio*, 395 U.S. 444 1969 Speech Imminent Lawless Action Established the modern standard that advocacy of illegal action can be punished only if it is directed to and likely to cause imminent lawless action.
*Cohen v. California*, 403 U.S. 15 1971 Speech Offensive Speech Protected offensive speech, ruling the state could not punish a man for wearing a jacket with "Fuck the Draft" in a courthouse.
*Lemon v. Kurtzman*, 403 U.S. 602 1971 Establishment *Lemon* Test Created the three-part *Lemon* test for Establishment Clause cases, adding an "excessive entanglement" prong.
*New York Times Co. v. United States*, 403 U.S. 713 1971 Press Prior Restraint Rejected the government's attempt to enjoin publication of the Pentagon Papers, affirming a "heavy presumption" against prior restraint.
*Wisconsin v. Yoder*, 406 U.S. 205 1972 Free Exercise Compelling Interest Test Granted Amish parents an exemption from compulsory education laws past the eighth grade.
*Branzburg v. Hayes*, 408 U.S. 665 1972 Press Reporter's Privilege Held that the First Amendment does not provide reporters with a privilege to refuse to testify before a grand jury.
*California Motor Transport Co. v. Trucking Unlimited*, 404 U.S. 508 1972 Petition Sham Litigation Held that the right to petition does not protect "sham" litigation used to harass competitors in violation of antitrust laws.
*Miller v. California*, 413 U.S. 15 1973 Speech Obscenity (*Miller* Test) Established the three-part test for determining whether material is obscene and unprotected.
*Gertz v. Robert Welch, Inc.*, 418 U.S. 323 1974 Speech, Press Defamation (Private Figures) Held that for private figure plaintiffs, states can set a negligence standard for defamation, but punitive damages require "actual malice."
*Spence v. Washington*, 418 U.S. 405 1974 Speech Expressive Conduct (*Spence* Test) Created a test to determine if conduct is expressive: intent to convey a message and likelihood it will be understood.
*Cox Broadcasting Corp. v. Cohn*, 420 U.S. 469 1975 Press, Speech Publishing Truthful Information Held that the press cannot be held liable for publishing a rape victim's name obtained from public records.
*Buckley v. Valeo*, 424 U.S. 1 1976 Speech Campaign Finance Upheld contribution limits but struck down expenditure limits in political campaigns, equating spending money with speech.
*Zurcher v. Stanford Daily*, 436 U.S. 547 1978 Press Newsroom Searches Ruled that the First Amendment does not prevent police from obtaining a warrant to search a newsroom.
*Bethel School District v. Fraser*, 478 U.S. 675 1986 Speech Student Speech Ruled schools can punish students for lewd and indecent speech that is inconsistent with the school's educational mission.
*Hustler Magazine, Inc. v. Falwell*, 485 U.S. 46 1988 Speech, Press Parody and Satire Held that public figures cannot recover for intentional infliction of emotional distress from a parody without showing a false statement of fact made with actual malice.
*Hazelwood School District v. Kuhlmeier*, 484 U.S. 260 1988 Speech, Press Student Speech (School-Sponsored) Ruled that schools can censor school-sponsored student expression if it is reasonably related to legitimate pedagogical concerns.
*Texas v. Johnson*, 491 U.S. 397 1989 Speech Symbolic Speech (Flag Burning) Ruled that burning the American flag is a form of expressive conduct protected by the First Amendment.
*The Florida Star v. B.J.F.*, 491 U.S. 524 1989 Press, Speech Publishing Truthful Information Held a newspaper could not be liable for publishing a rape victim's name it lawfully obtained from a police report.
*Employment Division v. Smith*, 494 U.S. 872 1990 Free Exercise Neutral Laws of General Applicability Held the Free Exercise Clause does not exempt individuals from neutral, generally applicable laws, even if they burden religious practice.
*R. A. V. v. City of St. Paul*, 505 U.S. 377 1992 Speech Content Discrimination (Hate Speech) Struck down a hate speech ordinance because it selectively banned "fighting words" based on their content (race, religion, etc.).
*Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye, Inc. v. City of Hialeah*, 508 U.S. 520 1993 Free Exercise Neutrality and General Applicability Struck down ordinances banning animal sacrifice because they were not neutral but targeted a specific religion.
*Reno v. ACLU*, 521 U.S. 844 1997 Speech Internet Speech Afforded speech on the internet the highest level of First Amendment protection, striking down the Communications Decency Act.
*Boy Scouts of America v. Dale*, 530 U.S. 640 2000 Assembly Expressive Association Upheld the Boy Scouts' right to exclude a gay scoutmaster, finding forced inclusion would impair the group's expressive message.
*Morse v. Frederick*, 551 U.S. 393 2007 Speech Student Speech (Drug-Related) Ruled schools can restrict student speech at school events that is reasonably viewed as promoting illegal drug use.
*Citizens United v. FEC*, 558 U.S. 310 2010 Speech Campaign Finance (Corporate Speech) Struck down prohibitions on independent expenditures by corporations and unions in elections.
*Snyder v. Phelps*, 562 U.S. 443 2011 Speech, Assembly Public Protest / Offensive Speech Protected the right of Westboro Baptist Church to picket a military funeral, shielding even "outrageous" speech on public issues.
*303 Creative LLC v. Elenis*, 599 U.S. \_\_\_ 2023 Speech Compelled Speech Held that the First Amendment prohibits a state from forcing a website designer to create expressive designs speaking messages with which the designer disagrees.
*Murthy v. Missouri*, 602 U.S. \_\_\_ 2024 Speech Government Coercion of Platforms Addressed claims of government coercion of social media platforms, clarifying the standard for when government communication becomes unconstitutional pressure.
*Moody v. NetChoice, LLC*, 602 U.S. \_\_\_ 2024 Speech, Press Social Media Regulation Held that social media platforms engage in protected speech and that state laws regulating their content moderation practices implicate the First Amendment.
*National Rifle Association v. Vullo*, 602 U.S. \_\_\_ 2024 Speech, Petition Government Coercion Held that a government official's coercive threats aimed at chilling a group's advocacy speech could state a plausible First Amendment claim.
*Cochran v. Louisiana State Board of Education*, 281 U.S. 370 1930 Establishment Government Aid Upheld providing secular textbooks to all students, including those in parochial schools, under a "child benefit" theory.
*Minersville School District v. Gobitis*, 310 U.S. 586 1940 Free Exercise, Speech Compelled Speech Upheld mandatory flag salutes in schools, a decision that was famously overruled three years later.
*Murdock v. Pennsylvania*, 319 U.S. 105 1943 Free Exercise, Speech Licensing and Fees Struck down a license tax on soliciting as applied to Jehovah's Witnesses distributing religious literature.
*Prince v. Massachusetts*, 321 U.S. 158 1944 Free Exercise Child Labor Laws Upheld child labor laws that prevented a Jehovah's Witness from having her young niece distribute religious literature.
*Marsh v. Alabama*, 326 U.S. 501 1946 Speech, Religion Company Town as Public Forum Ruled that a privately owned "company town" could not prohibit the distribution of religious literature on its sidewalks.
*Burstyn v. Wilson*, 343 U.S. 495 1952 Speech, Press Film Censorship Held that films are a significant medium for the communication of ideas and are protected by the First Amendment.
*Watkins v. United States*, 354 U.S. 178 1957 Speech Congressional Investigations Curbed the powers of the House Un-American Activities Committee on First Amendment grounds.
*Speiser v. Randall*, 357 U.S. 513 1958 Speech Loyalty Oaths Struck down a California law requiring veterans to sign a loyalty oath to receive a property tax exemption.
*Braunfeld v. Brown*, 366 U.S. 599 1961 Free Exercise Blue Laws Upheld a Sunday closing law ("blue law") against a challenge from Orthodox Jewish merchants.
*Keyishian v. Board of Regents*, 385 U.S. 589 1967 Speech Loyalty Oaths (Academic Freedom) Struck down a state law requiring public school teachers to sign a loyalty oath.
*Epperson v. Arkansas*, 393 U.S. 97 1968 Establishment Teaching Evolution Struck down an Arkansas law that banned the teaching of evolution in public schools.
*Healy v. James*, 408 U.S. 169 1972 Assembly, Speech Student Groups (College) Held that a public college could not deny official recognition to a student group simply because of its affiliation with a national organization.
*Virginia State Pharmacy Board v. Virginia Citizens Consumer Council*, 425 U.S. 748 1976 Speech Commercial Speech Established that purely commercial speech enjoys First Amendment protection.
*Wooley v. Maynard*, 430 U.S. 705 1977 Speech Compelled Speech ("Right Not to Speak") Held that New Hampshire could not force a Jehovah's Witness to display the state motto "Live Free or Die" on his license plate.
*First National Bank of Boston v. Bellotti*, 435 U.S. 765 1978 Speech Corporate Political Speech Struck down a state law banning corporate expenditures related to ballot initiatives.
*Central Hudson Gas & Elec. v. Public Service Comm'n*, 447 U.S. 557 1980 Speech Commercial Speech (*Central Hudson* Test) Established a four-part test for determining the constitutionality of restrictions on commercial speech.
*Stone v. Graham*, 449 U.S. 39 1980 Establishment Ten Commandments in Schools Struck down a Kentucky law requiring the posting of the Ten Commandments in public school classrooms.
*Heffron v. Int'l Soc. for Krishna Consciousness*, 452 U.S. 640 1981 Speech, Religion Time, Place, and Manner Upheld a state fair rule limiting solicitation to fixed booths as a valid time, place, and manner restriction.
*Island Trees School District v. Pico*, 457 U.S. 853 1982 Speech School Libraries Held that school boards cannot remove books from school libraries simply because they dislike the ideas contained in them.
*Wallace v. Jaffree*, 472 U.S. 38 1985 Establishment Moment of Silence Struck down an Alabama law authorizing a one-minute period of silence for "meditation or voluntary prayer" as lacking a secular purpose.
*Edwards v. Aguillard*, 482 U.S. 578 1987 Establishment Teaching Creationism Struck down a Louisiana law requiring "balanced treatment" for creation science and evolution.
*Westside Community Board of Ed. v. Mergens*, 496 U.S. 226 1990 Speech, Religion Equal Access Act Upheld the Equal Access Act, which requires public secondary schools to allow student religious groups to meet if other non-curriculum groups can.
*Lee v. Weisman*, 505 U.S. 577 1992 Establishment Graduation Prayer Ruled that clergy-led prayer at a public school graduation ceremony is unconstitutional.
*Lamb's Chapel v. Center Moriches Union Free School Dist.*, 508 U.S. 384 1993 Speech, Religion Viewpoint Discrimination Held that a school district could not deny a church access to school premises to show a film series on family values when it allowed other groups to use the premises for social or civic purposes.
*Rosenberger v. University of Virginia*, 515 U.S. 819 1995 Speech, Religion Viewpoint Discrimination Ruled that a public university could not deny funding to a student religious publication while funding other student publications.
*Agostini v. Felton*, 521 U.S. 203 1997 Establishment Government Aid Overturned an earlier decision, allowing public school teachers to provide remedial education in parochial schools.
*Santa Fe Independent School Dist. v. Doe*, 530 U.S. 290 2000 Establishment Student-Led Prayer at Games Ruled that student-led, student-initiated prayer over the public address system at public school football games is unconstitutional.
*Good News Club v. Milford Central School*, 533 U.S. 98 2001 Speech, Religion Viewpoint Discrimination Held that a school could not exclude a Christian children's club from meeting after hours based on the religious nature of its speech.
*Zelman v. Simmons-Harris*, 536 U.S. 639 2002 Establishment School Vouchers Upheld a school voucher program in Cleveland, finding it was neutral toward religion and based on private choice.
*Watchtower Society v. Village of Stratton*, 536 U.S. 150 2002 Speech, Religion Door-to-Door Solicitation Struck down a village ordinance requiring a permit for door-to-door advocacy as a violation of free speech rights.
*Van Orden v. Perry*, 545 U.S. 677 2005 Establishment Ten Commandments Display Allowed a Ten Commandments monument on the grounds of the Texas state capitol, finding it had historical as well as religious significance.
*McCreary County v. ACLU*, 545 U.S. 844 2005 Establishment Ten Commandments Display Struck down displays of the Ten Commandments in two Kentucky courthouses, finding they had a predominantly religious purpose.
*Gonzales v. O Centro Espirita*, 546 U.S. 418 2006 Free Exercise Religious Freedom Restoration Act (RFRA) Held that under RFRA, the government had not shown a compelling interest in banning a small religious sect's sacramental use of a hallucinogenic tea.
*Pleasant Grove City v. Summum*, 555 U.S. 460 2009 Speech Government Speech Doctrine Ruled that a city's placement of permanent monuments in a public park is a form of government speech and not subject to free speech claims from other groups.
*Hosanna-Tabor Evangelical Lutheran Church & School v. EEOC*, 565 U.S. 171 2012 Religion Clauses Ministerial Exception Recognized a "ministerial exception" to employment discrimination laws, protecting a religious organization's right to choose its ministers.
*Town of Greece v. Galloway*, 572 U.S. 565 2014 Establishment Legislative Prayer Upheld a town's practice of opening board meetings with a prayer, even if the prayers were predominantly Christian.
*Burwell v. Hobby Lobby Stores, Inc.*, 573 U.S. 682 2014 Free Exercise RFRA (Corporate Persons) Held that under RFRA, closely held for-profit corporations could receive an exemption from a federal mandate on religious grounds.
*Matal v. Tam*, 582 U.S. \_\_\_ 2017 Speech Government Speech / Viewpoint Discrimination Struck down a federal law prohibiting the registration of "disparaging" trademarks, finding it to be unconstitutional viewpoint discrimination.
*Trinity Lutheran Church v. Comer*, 582 U.S. \_\_\_ 2017 Free Exercise Exclusion from Public Benefits Ruled that a state could not exclude a church from a generally available public benefit program (playground resurfacing) solely because of its religious character.
*American Legion v. American Humanist Association*, 588 U.S. \_\_\_ 2019 Establishment Religious Displays Allowed a large cross-shaped war memorial to remain on public land, moving away from the *Lemon* test for long-standing monuments.
*Iancu v. Brunetti*, 588 U.S. \_\_\_ 2019 Speech Viewpoint Discrimination Struck down a federal law prohibiting the registration of "immoral" or "scandalous" trademarks as unconstitutional viewpoint discrimination.
*Espinoza v. Montana Dept. of Revenue*, 591 U.S. \_\_\_ 2020 Free Exercise Exclusion from Public Benefits Ruled that a state cannot exclude religious schools from a private school choice program once it creates such a program.
*Fulton v. City of Philadelphia*, 593 U.S. \_\_\_ 2021 Free Exercise Neutrality and General Applicability Ruled that Philadelphia could not refuse to contract with a Catholic social services agency that would not certify same-sex couples as foster parents, because the city's non-discrimination policy was not "generally applicable."
*Mahanoy Area School Dist. v. B.L.*, 594 U.S. \_\_\_ 2021 Speech Student Speech (Off-Campus) Ruled that a school violated a student's First Amendment rights by punishing her for off-campus speech (a profane Snapchat post).
*Kennedy v. Bremerton School District*, 597 U.S. \_\_\_ 2022 Speech, Free Exercise Public Employee Religious Speech Protected the right of a high school football coach to engage in personal prayer on the field after games.
*Carson v. Makin*, 596 U.S. \_\_\_ 2022 Free Exercise Exclusion from Public Benefits Ruled that a state that provides tuition assistance for private schools cannot exclude schools that provide religious instruction.
